# Research type and interactive development

Use this reference at setup, when the audience or intended decision changes, or when deciding which topics to open next. Research type and interaction mode are separate: every type can prepare, synthesize, or continue, interactively or as a direct deliverable.

## Establish the research type

Use explicit user context first. If the type is consequential and unclear, ask who the service is for or offer ToC, ToB/G, and mixed/not yet clear as choices. Reuse known context instead of making the user classify it again.

| Type | Research emphasis | Typical output emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| ToC | A person's context, trigger, recent behavior, alternatives, choice, effort, trust, motivation, purchase/use/return or abandonment | Evidence-based segments, a concrete user journey, friction and unmet needs, opportunity hypotheses and validation questions |
| ToB/G | Concrete jobs, responsibilities, end-to-end work, handoffs, approval authority, rules, data, system boundaries, control requirements, implementation and adoption constraints | Business-topic notes, workflows and applicable approval rules, organization differences, product-fit conditions, materials and unresolved decisions |
| Mixed / B2B2C / public-facing service | The consumer or resident journey and the organization's delivery/operating process, with their shared states and handoffs | Separate views for the relevant participants plus their connection; do not merge their needs into one persona or one approval workflow |

These are lenses, not exclusive checklists. ToC can involve eligibility reviews or backend approvals. ToB/G also requires understanding motivation, usability, trust, and willingness or ability to adopt. Do not equate an individual employee using an app with ToC, or a state-owned enterprise with ToG.

Within ToB/G retain the distinction when it matters:

- **ToB:** understand the customer organization, its decision-maker, buyer, operators, and adoption responsibilities. Budget or purchasing questions matter when scope, rollout, or purchase is the decision; do not turn every internal workflow interview into sales qualification.
- **ToG:** identify the commissioning/operating public body, frontline staff, service recipients, policy authority, and accountability. Investigate public-service outcomes, accessibility, information responsibilities, procurement/budget milestones, or policy constraints only when they affect the task. Cite the applicable supplied or verified policy; a stakeholder's account does not establish a legal requirement.

If the user changes the type, retain earlier facts and reframe only affected questions, audience, and outputs. Mark the old classification as superseded, not false evidence.

## Agree a small starting scope

1. Read known material. State the current understanding in a few lines: research type, intended decision, relevant participants, and what is already known.
2. Suggest a short outline for this round. For example, start a ToC case with context → last attempt → friction; start an enterprise case with a concrete business → its handoffs → the consequential rules. Leave later topics as proposed.
3. Ask only the unresolved choice needed to start. If type is unknown, settle that before requesting a detailed outline decision. If the user already selected a topic, begin there. Use the host's interactive question UI when available; otherwise ask a concise plain-text question. Do not depend on a particular UI tool.
4. Distinguish a research-planning choice from a factual interview question. A recommended next topic is helpful; a recommended answer about the customer's actual behavior is leading.

The outline is adjustable. It is neither a commitment to complete every topic nor a set of blanks to fill with guesses. If the user asks only for a proposed structure, show it and stop at that scope.

## Expand one topic through dialogue

- Ask one main question. Use the answer to decide whether a dependent question is still necessary. Avoid one sentence containing several questions.
- Capture a small usable topic draft as answers arrive: known situation/behavior, friction, desired outcome, evidence limits, and product implications if there is evidence for them. Do not manufacture a finding to populate every field.
- Briefly reflect consequential understanding for correction. Ask for confirmation where an interpretation changes scope, a rule, or a fit judgment. User confirmation of the summary means the summary reflects their account; it does not verify customer policy, production behavior, or acceptance.
- A topic can be sufficiently understood for this round, open, awaiting another respondent, deferred, or outside scope. Unknown answers should lead to a suitable evidence request or respondent, not repeated pressure on the same user.
- After the core case and consequential variants are covered, recommend the next topic or offer to summarize. Do not keep probing merely because more questions are conceivable.
- If the user asks to skip, stop, or get an interim result, preserve known information and list the remaining gaps. Do not require another confirmation round first.

No response is not approval. While a question is pending, organize existing evidence or prepare independent work, but do not answer the question on the user's behalf or move through its dependent branch.

## Keep continuation lightweight

Maintain a short session state in the working brief when one is saved:

`type and participants | agreed decision/scope | interaction mode | active topic | covered/open/deferred topics | next unresolved question`

Keep source and question IDs stable. On resume, acknowledge the active topic and ask only its unresolved question. Do not repeat an answered setup questionnaire. A change from interactive to direct output changes the delivery mode, not the evidence status.

For a one-shot guide or synthesis, use the same type-specific emphasis, produce the requested artifact, and record assumptions or gaps. An interactive default must not block an explicit direct-output request.
