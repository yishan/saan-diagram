# Research artifacts

Scale the artifact to the request. One Markdown brief is sufficient for a small scope. Create a separate customer guide when its audience or use differs. Follow existing file conventions; otherwise use `discovery-<topic>.md` and, when useful, `interview-guide-<topic>.md` in the agreed working directory. Do not create empty files for every possible output.

For a short input or interim brief, combine overlapping sections. Record a common evidence limitation once and refer to it; do not repeat the same facts and unknowns in a source ledger, workflow table, fit table, gap table, and conclusion. Keep the few consequential findings and next questions easy to find. Expand detail where the supplied material or requested decision requires it, not to fill the outline below.

## Build the brief by topic

In interactive mode, start with a short agreed outline and expand only the active topic as evidence arrives. Use [Research context and interaction](research-context.md) for type selection, confirmation, and session state. Unopened topics are unexamined; skipped topics are deferred or outside scope. Neither means the corresponding behavior is absent.

For each substantive topic, combine the known situation or behavior, actual journey/process, friction, expectations, relevant product evidence, and unresolved questions in concise prose or a small table. Clearly separate participant accounts from the researcher's interpretation. Omit unsupported or irrelevant fields rather than padding the topic.

- **ToC:** organize around the person in a context and their journey, meaningful alternatives, observed friction, desired outcomes, and opportunity hypotheses. Add segment comparisons or measurements only where supported. Do not impose an enterprise approval/authority template.
- **ToB/G:** organize around concrete business topics and responsible roles. Explain current practice, workflow, applicable approval/rule details, friction, expectations, fit conditions, and records to obtain. Across enterprises or departments, summarize common needs while preserving differences and their applicable scope. For ToG, keep service-recipient outcomes distinct from administrative acceptance.
- **Mixed:** preserve connected but separate user/recipient and organization views. Identify shared objects, states, and handoffs; an internal approval is not proof of external service completion.

Put a short findings summary first when useful. Keep detailed source/claim ledgers in an appendix or working section so the main account remains readable. Consequential claims still need a nearby source locator; do not sacrifice traceability for a cleaner layout.

## Supporting coverage

Use these sections where relevant. Concise tables may combine fields without losing evidence or uncertainty.

1. **Purpose and scope:** research type, intended decision, customer/business scope, stage, participants and roles, time constraints, product/version, material limits; interactive session state where needed.
2. **Sources and terms:** stable source IDs and locators; customer-to-product term mappings, unresolved meanings.
3. **Current behavior or business:** context, alternatives, objects, step sequence, touchpoints or handoffs, and completion/abandonment states; approval rules recorded separately when applicable. Distinguish policy and practice.
4. **Friction and expectations:** affected step and role, impact evidence, frequency/measurement limits, current remedy; desired result, priority and acceptance direction.
5. **Product-fit assessment:** customer condition, product evidence, category, evidence boundary, consequence and verification.
6. **Gaps and next actions:** question IDs, intended respondent or unassigned owner, prerequisites, impact, requested evidence, status and agreed action. Include conflicts.
7. **Stage conclusion:** what the material enables, which decisions remain blocked, and what the next research step would resolve.

### Source and claim ledger

Use stable identifiers such as S1 for a source and F1 for a consequential claim. Not every sentence needs a separate ID. Each important claim should be traceable without searching a whole transcript.

`Claim ID | statement | category | source + locator | evidence status | scope/limit`

Content category: current practice, policy, desired outcome, product design, product implementation/configuration, recommendation.

Evidence status: observed/record-backed; participant report; inference; conflict; unknown. A record proves only the event and scope it actually records. Mark exact quotations; label paraphrases as such. Dates and personal details absent from the material are not invented.

### Gap queue

`Question ID | gap / neutral question | affected decision | respondent | prerequisite | useful evidence | status`

Statuses can be: open, answered but unverified, evidence checked, conflicting, awaiting another role, explicitly deferred, or not applicable with reason. Use local-language labels. A proposed role is not an agreed assignment; an inferred deadline is not a commitment.

## Customer-facing interview guide

Include purpose, audience, minimal context, and the prioritized questions for that audience. Match the user's time budget. Separate must-ask questions from conditional probes and evidence requests. Each question has one main idea; answer space is useful for an asynchronous questionnaire.

Keep internal product-fit hypotheses, risk rankings, and recommended business rules out of the customer's factual questions. Explain why a question matters only when it helps the person answer. Offer partial answers and explicit unknowns as valid responses.

## Resume and update

1. Read the existing brief immediately before changing it.
2. Add new sources using stable IDs. Preserve earlier sources and unrelated content.
3. Resolve, narrow, or reopen affected questions with the new source and scope. Retain a short resolution history for material changes.
4. Reassess only affected process, rule, and fit conclusions. Do not re-ask answered questions without a new discrepancy or changed scope.
5. List residual gaps separately from resolved ones. If a question remains partly open, name the unresolved part. Preserve the agreed type, scope, active topic, and deferred topics so continuation does not restart setup.

## Pre-delivery review

- Can each consequential rule, number, and fit claim be traced to its evidence or clearly labeled as a hypothesis?
- Are actual practice, policy, desired behavior, and product behavior still distinct?
- Does approval completion remain distinct from business completion?
- Do conflicts retain both accounts and a way to check them?
- Are questions aimed at the missing information and the right role, without invented assignments?
- Is the output useful at the requested stage, with uncertainty and limitations explicit?

These checks assess artifact quality. They do not establish that the customer accepted the rules or that the product has been verified in production.
