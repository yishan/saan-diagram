Saan Discovery

Agree who and what to study, then explore real behavior, business workflows, and product fit one topic at a time.

For
Consumer, enterprise, and public-sector research
Outputs
Interview guides, research briefs, follow-ups, and fit assessments
Dependencies
Text-only skill; no external service required

Match the research to the people involved

ContextWhat to exploreHow to organize the findings
Consumer · ToCContext, motivation, recent behavior, alternatives, and reasons for stoppingUser journeys, behavioral differences, friction, and opportunity hypotheses
Enterprise and public sector · ToB/GRoles, handoffs, applicable approval rules, data, systems, and implementation constraintsBusiness topics, organization differences, fit conditions, and open decisions
MixedThe consumer or resident experience and how the organization delivers the serviceSeparate but connected user journeys and organizational processes

Preserve enterprise and public-sector distinctions. Public-service research may need service-recipient outcomes, policy sources, and accountability. State ownership alone does not make a business ToG.

Confirm unresolved scope first, then develop one topic and one main question at a time. Correct, skip, or request an interim brief whenever needed. An explicit request for direct synthesis produces a complete response from the available evidence.

Support the full discovery cycle

  1. Prepare the interview

    Read the product material. Prepare essential questions and conditional probes for the participants and available time.

  2. Synthesize the evidence

    Organize current behavior, friction, and expectations by journey or business topic. Preserve sources and conflicting accounts.

  3. Close consequential gaps

    Route unanswered questions to suitable people. Update the existing brief with new answers and retain resolved questions.

Turn a broad complaint into a concrete case

“Payment approval is slow. We need more flexibility.”
  1. Agree whether this round concerns the payment workflow or approval authority and rules.
  2. If the workflow is selected, ask: how did a recent payment request reach its current state?
  3. Use the answer to decide whether to probe a waiting point and its causes.

The answer determines the next question. Missing documents, queues, unclear responsibilities, and system effort call for different evidence.

Fictional example. Interview methods are bundled inside the skill; no separate user-interview installation is required.

Use product logic to identify essential questions

Product logicCustomer conditions to establishDecision affected
Approval routing by amount and organizationAmount definitions, boundaries, organization meaning, and exception authorityWhether configuration can express the actual rules
A defined business state flowWhen changes take effect and what happens after returns or amendmentsWhether states and actions map to the business
Data supplied by another systemWho maintains it, when it updates, and who handles failuresIntegration scope and data responsibility

Keep customer-specific tasks and offline work in scope. Missing product material does not block discovery; the affected fit judgments remain unverified.

Keep evidence close to each conclusion

  • Record current practice, policy requirements, and desired outcomes separately.
  • Distinguish approval, business execution, and reconciliation.
  • Preserve both sources when roles disagree, along with the action needed to verify the claim.
  • State what product documentation, environment settings, and observed runs each establish.
  • Turn unknown answers into follow-ups. A fit gap does not become a development commitment.

Get started

Install just Saan Discovery from Saan Kits:

npx skills add yishan/saankits --skill saan-discovery

Then give your agent the product material and customer context:

Use saan-discovery to agree the research type and scope with me, then develop one topic at a time. Ask one main question per turn and keep unknown facts open.

Questions and deliverables follow your requested language, or the language of your request when none is specified. A request in Chinese receives Chinese output by default.

Fictional simulations cover interview preparation, synthesis, follow-up evidence, and missing product material. Effectiveness in real customer interviews still needs field validation.
