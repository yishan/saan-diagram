Saan Inquiry

Think through an unclear problem, examine what you may be missing, and decide a useful next step.

Use for
Problem clarification, possible causes, and difficult choices
Outputs
A clearer understanding, provisional judgment, or action with a review condition
Dependencies
Text-only dialogue; no external service or peer skill required

Choose what helps the conversation move forward

  • Ask a focused question when its answer would change the judgment or next action.
  • Offer a concrete interpretation or an alternative when that is easier to work with.
  • Introduce a relevant blind spot as a possibility to examine, with a reason it matters.
  • Synthesize when enough is known, you want to proceed, or a small trial would teach more.

You can correct, skip, say “I don't know,” or ask for a provisional answer. There is no questionnaire to complete.

A better explanation can change with the evidence

Fictional example · Team delivery

“Reviews are faster now, but delivery still takes just as long.”

The intervention improved review waiting. That alone did not resolve the original delay. Rework, changed workload, or another handoff may matter; the result does not yet establish which.

A useful next move is to inspect where the remaining time goes, then revise the explanation. Repeating the original questions would add little.

Make the next step worth taking

At a useful stopping point

State the current understanding, material unknowns, and a feasible next action or observation. A simple clarification can end with one sentence.

When results return

Compare what happened with the original expectation. Decide what is resolved, what needs adjustment, and what remains uncertain. A plan is not a completed validation.

Causes can interact. The skill does not promise one ultimate root cause, every blind spot, or certainty where evidence is missing.

Useful beyond customer interviews

Use it for product ideas, team problems, learning, and everyday choices. For a customer research guide, see Saan Discovery; for a requirements document, see Saan PRD Writing. Neither is a dependency.

The working name was Questinoare. Saan Inquiry describes open exploration without implying a fixed questionnaire. Startup interview prompts about real customers, use, payments, and delivery are examples, not a compulsory checklist.

Try the local draft

From a Saan Kits checkout containing this skill:

npx skills add . --skill saan-inquiry

Start with the problem in your own words:

Use saan-inquiry to help me clarify this problem, examine what I may be missing, and decide a useful next step. Ask only what would change our understanding or action.
